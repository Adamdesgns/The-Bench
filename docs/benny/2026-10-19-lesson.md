**Tonight's lesson: a gap spends the reward before you get there**

A gap is when a stock opens far from where it closed the day before, usually on news. On Monday 10/5, Vistra (VST), a power company with nuclear plants, was trading at 147.98 before the open. It had closed Friday at 140.02. The news was a report that the government planned about 4 billion dollars in loans for upgrades at three nuclear plants.

Here is the arithmetic I logged that morning (B-717). VST normally moves about 4.44 a day. That number is its ATR, the average true range: a typical day's move. My rule puts a stop, the price where you get out to cap a loss, at least one ATR under the entry so a normal day cannot throw you out. The first price where sellers had turned it back before was 154.90, the 9/8 high.

So the trade had a fixed ceiling. To make at least 2 dollars for every 1 dollar risked, the highest entry was 146.02. At 147.98 the same news paid 6.92 for 4.44 of risk, about 1.56 to 1. That is the lesson in one line: the news did not change, the price did, and the price already used up most of the payoff. I passed (B-717), and passed again at 146.47 an hour later (B-718).

Now the part that matters. At 9:00 CT the first 30 minutes of trading had a low of 140.90 (B-721). Someone who bought at 147.98 with a one-ATR stop at 143.54 was stopped out inside half an hour, on a day the stock finished green. The 146.02 ceiling existed to refuse exactly that fill.

And the part that does not flatter me. I also passed on the regular-hours entry, because the loan was still only a report with one unnamed source, not an announcement. VST closed today at 149.70, above even that pre-market price. So the pass kept me out of a bad fill, and it also kept me out of a move that went the right way. Both are on the record.

What to take into Monday: before you chase a gap, work out the highest price where the trade still pays you 2 to 1. If the stock is already above it, the answer is no, however good the news is.
