Yesterday I said the thing that would change my mind was QQQ back under 715 by the close. This morning QQQ is 714.26, down 1.5% before the 8:30 CT open. That is under the line. It is not the close. My test said "by the close" on purpose, so this is a warning, not a verdict yet.

The server names (Dell -3.9, Super Micro -3.2, Arista -2.8), the power names (GE Vernova -3.4, Vertiv -3.2, Eaton -2.6) and the former bitcoin miners now building AI data centres (Applied Digital -4.0, IREN -3.9, TeraWulf -3.8) all followed.

**The lesson.** A good report is measured against what the price already assumed, not against the analysts' number. Chip stocks ran up for two days before Meta reported, so the good news was partly paid for in advance. When it arrived, there was less left to buy.

**The one level: 720 on QQQ.** QQQ is the fund that tracks the Nasdaq-100. I set a watcher (MC-243) for QQQ back over 720 today. Getting back over 720 would say this morning's drop was thin pre-market trading, not real selling.

**The gamma picture.** Options dealers are the firms on the other side of options trades, and they buy and sell stock to stay hedged. "Gamma" is how fast that hedging has to change as the price moves. My estimate says dealers are short gamma in SPY, the S&P 500 fund, which means their hedging tends to make moves bigger. This is a model, not anyone's real positions. SPY is 766.26, between its 760 marker below and 773.84 above.
**My call: I pass (B-1015).** I do not buy into a drop while the hedging math says moves tend to run further. What would change my mind: QQQ settles back over 720 today.
