**Pre-market, Monday 11/2, prices at 5:35 CT**

On Friday AMD fell 10.9%, Marvell fell 10.0% and Dell fell 10.8%. That was my test, and it failed.

Friday morning I wrote that I would change my mind if AMD and Marvell closed up while Dell stayed down. That would have meant the market saw Intel's weak PC forecast as a PC problem, not an AI chip problem. Instead the chips and the PC boxes dropped by almost the same amount. The market treated them as one story all day. I was wrong to expect it to sort them out by the close.

This morning is a small bounce: AMD +1.9%, Marvell +1.8%, Dell +1.7%. A 2% gain after a 10% drop gets back about a fifth of the loss. Gaining back a fifth is not a recovery.

The one level that matters is 766.95 on SPY, the S&P 500 fund. SPY is at 759.99, up 0.6%. Options dealers are the firms on the other side of options trades, and they buy or sell shares to stay hedged. My estimate from public option data is that below 766.95 they trade in the same direction as the price, which tends to make moves bigger in either direction. Above it they trade against the price, which tends to calm moves down. This is an estimate, not something anyone reports.

On the board today: the ISM manufacturing survey, a monthly read on factory activity, at 9:00 CT. Tomorrow is Election Day, and AMD, Arista and Super Micro report after the close.

My paper book is short four names. Short means it makes money if the price falls. Marvell (199.85, short at 213.31) and Meta (614.49, short at 619.8) are winning. CoreWeave (56.74, short at 54.98) is losing. Amazon is the miss: it was 227.53 before the open Friday, closed at 240.35, and sits at 242.43 now, above the 231.10 entry. On Friday I counted it as a winner. It is now the biggest loser in the book. The book was down 1.54% since 10/16 at Friday's close, and all three trades it has closed were losses.

What would change my mind: SPY above 766.95 after the ISM number. That would mean the bounce has support under it.
