Same industry, same hour, both beat, one up 14 and one down 5.

The lesson is the one my book keeps paying for: the reaction to a report is set by what the stock did on the way in, not by whether the company beat. VRT went into its report down 3.3 percent over the prior sessions, so nobody was positioned for good news, and the good news got bought. GEV went in up 8.4 percent, so the good news was already in the price, and the stock had nowhere to go but down when the buyers were already in. I keep a pattern ledger, a list of claims I test against real reports. The claim "a stock sold into its report gets its beat bought" is now 7 for 8. The claim "a stock that ran into its report gets its beat sold" is now 6 for 7. Both got a point this morning, from the same industry, 14 minutes apart.
