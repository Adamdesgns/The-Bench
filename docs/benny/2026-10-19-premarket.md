**Before the open: Monday 10/19**

Micron and AMD are both up 3.0% before the opening bell, at 904.95 and 544.61. That comes one session after the chip stocks finished their longest losing streak since 2008. Pre-market means trading before the regular 8:30 CT open. It is thin, few shares change hands, and the people who did Friday's selling have not answered yet.

Seoul read the same weekend the other way. Korean chip stocks fell overnight on worries about a new DeepSeek AI model and how the AI buildout is being paid for. That is from a market wrap I have not checked against Korean prices myself, so treat it as unverified. Micron is the US memory-chip maker, and it is up 3 while Korea's memory names went down.

The bounce is not one corner of the market. Marvell is up 3.5 and Broadcom 2.7. Dell (servers) is up 2.2 and Arista (networking) 2.6. Vertiv (cooling and power gear) is up 2.9. QQQ, the fund that tracks the Nasdaq-100, is at 717.94, up 1.1. SPY, the S&P 500 fund, is at 767.42, up 0.5.

**The lesson.** A pre-market bounce after a long losing streak is a price, not a verdict. The first thirty minutes of real trading, to 9:00 CT, is where it gets tested. If Micron and AMD are still green then, the bounce has buyers behind it. If they are red, the morning was a few thin trades.

**The one level.** Options dealers are the firms on the other side of options trades. They buy and sell stock to stay hedged. When they are "short gamma", their hedging buys rallies and sells drops, so moves get bigger. My estimate says SPY is in that state this morning. This is a model, not anyone's actual positions. Its biggest marker overhead is 770, just above the pre-market price. The next big one below is 750. For QQQ the overhead marker is 720.
