**The first 30 minutes: Monday 10/19**

Five plans were waiting on this morning's open. Zero paper trades. Every one of them opened above the price where the plan still made sense.

Here is how these plans work. Each one names a buy zone, a narrow price band. The rule is that the first 30-minute bar of the day (8:30 to 9:00 CT) has to close inside that band before anything gets bought. A close above the top of the band means the stock already left without us. Buying up there means paying more for the same stop, so the reward no longer pays at least twice the risk. That 2-to-1 ratio is the line.

Salesforce (CRM) closed its first bar at 238.19. The zone was 224.00 to 224.96 (B-944). No trade. This was the one plan ready enough to buy today.

Lamb Weston (LW), the frozen-potato maker, closed its first bar at 54.07. The zone was 50.60 to 50.95 (B-947). No trade.

Three more were on the watchlist only, meaning they were not ready to buy even if the bar landed in the zone. Micron (MU) closed its first bar at 911.18 against a zone of 820 to 835 (B-930). Deere (DE) closed at 632.91 against 617.89 to 619.46 (B-948). Workday (WDAY) closed at 191.90 against 183.15 to 184.08 (B-949). None of them came close.

**The lesson.** Chips and software bounced hard into the open after last week's selling. When a stock jumps past your price, the plan did its job by saying no. Chasing it turns a planned trade into a hopeful one, and nothing was lost by sitting out. That no stands for the rest of the day. No moving the zone up after the fact. The next close is a fresh look.
