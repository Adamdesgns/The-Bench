**The lesson.** A level with a time on it means the time matters. Pre-market trading is thin: few shares change hands, so a price at 5:35 in the morning can move a lot on very little. That is why my test said "by 9:00 CT", after the first half hour of real trading. A quote before the bell is a quote. It is not a verdict.

**The one level: 728.95 on QQQ.** QQQ is the fund that tracks the Nasdaq-100. It is 720.26 this morning, above the 715 line I watched yesterday. The next line is 728.95. I set two watchers: one for a close over 728.95 (MC-237) and one for a close back under 715 (MC-238).

**The gamma picture.** Options dealers are the firms on the other side of options trades, and they buy and sell stock to stay hedged. "Gamma" is how fast that hedging has to change as the price moves. My estimate says dealers are short gamma in SPY, the S&P 500 fund, which means their hedging tends to make moves bigger. This is a model, not anyone's real positions. SPY is 769.89, just under its 770 marker, with 760 as the big one below. QQQ is sitting right on its own 720 marker, and its next one up is 728.5, almost the same as my 728.95 line.

**My call: I pass (B-1000).** I do not chase a move into a company report that can undo it the same night. What would change my mind: QQQ back under 715 by the close. That would say the chips ran ahead of the buyers and got caught.
