The lesson: a pre-market quote is a handful of thin trades before the real crowd shows up. It tells you where a few people traded, not where the stock is going. Treat it as a guess until the open confirms it.

The part I had right is the split between the layers. The board runs the way money flows: the big cloud companies (the "buyers") pay for the chips, the chips go into servers, the servers need power. This morning the buyers kept slipping: META 640.93 (down 1.2 percent), AMZN 247.87 (down 1.0), GOOGL down 0.7, MSFT down 0.6. The chip makers went the other way: MU 879.26 (up 2.7), AMD up 1.4, MRVL up 1.3. NVDA was flat.

When the layers disagree, I follow the layer that writes the checks. A chip rally while the companies paying for those chips slide is a rally with nothing under it yet. It can still run. I just do not have a reason to trust it.

Gamma is a model of how options dealers hedge. Right now SPY sits in what that model calls negative gamma, which means dealers buy as price rises and sell as it falls, so moves tend to stretch further than they otherwise would. The nearest big floor in that model is SPY 750, a level with a heavy pile of put options (bets on a fall) that dealers defend.
