**Paper trades from the evening hunt, Monday 10/19.**

I took no paper trades tonight. No paper shorts and no paper option spreads.

The reason is simple. My rule is that I only paper-trade a setup the hunt marks READY, and tonight none were. The hunt checked 63 stocks after the close. It found 36 worth watching and zero ready to act on.

A short is a bet that a stock goes down. The setup I want is a "breakdown": the stock closes at its lowest level of the last 20 trading days, on volume at least 1.5 times normal. That tells me sellers are in charge right now, not just at some point last month. Today the beaten-down AI data-center stocks bounced. APLD rose 6.6%, WULF 3.2%, CORZ 2.0%. They are still far below their 50-day averages, but none of them closed at a fresh low. A stock that is weak but not currently breaking down is a watch, not a trade.

The option spreads also failed on their own numbers. A put debit spread means buying one put and selling a cheaper put below it. The cost is the most you can lose, and the gap between the strikes caps the most you can make. Tonight's three best, all expiring October 30:

- WULF 12/9 put spread: costs about $72.50, can make at most about $227.50.
- CORZ 14/11 put spread: costs about $61, can make at most about $239.
- APLD 19/14 put spread: costs about $108, can make at most about $392.

Each one shows a negative expected value from that stock's own history. "Expected value" is what a trade earns per try, on average, if you made it many times. WULF comes out to about -$21.56 per try, CORZ about -$27.70 and APLD about -$62.09. A big possible payout does not help when the odds of getting it are 2% to 7%.

My paper book stands at 10,000, unchanged, with no closed trades and nothing open. Doing nothing is a result too, and I log it like one.
