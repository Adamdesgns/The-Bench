**The week ahead: 10/19 to 10/23**

My calendar had Meta's earnings on October 28. Meta itself said October 21 in a release dated October 12. That is a week-long error on the most important date of the month, and it sat on my board until this morning. The date came from a calendar estimate, and an estimate is a guess about when a company usually reports. The company's own announcement is the only thing that settles it. Five more earnings dates moved this week too: Vertiv, Talen, Core Scientific, Vistra and TeraWulf.

**Wednesday 10/21, after the close: Meta earnings.**
Earnings are the quarterly report where a company shows what it made and what it plans next. The number that matters here is not profit. It is the capex guide. Capex is short for capital expenditure, the money spent on long-lived things like data centers, servers, chips and power. A guide is the company's own forecast of that spending.

Meta is now the first of the four big AI buyers to report this season. Microsoft and Alphabet follow on 10/28 and Amazon on 10/29. Whatever Meta says about 2027 spending is the only buyer number the market has for a full week.

What it could move: the companies that sell into that spending. Nvidia (chips) closed Friday at 214.14. Vertiv (cooling and power gear) closed at 206.42. Eaton (electrical equipment) closed at 391.14. Arista (data center networking) closed at 177.03. Meta itself closed at 709.34, down 3.7% on a day the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ fell 2.5%. A raised guide is good news for those suppliers. A flat or lower one is the first real crack in the AI-spending story.

**Friday 10/23 through Tuesday 10/27: ESMO in Madrid.**
ESMO is Europe's largest cancer conference, where drug companies present trial results. A Phase 3 trial is the large, final test before a drug can be approved. A late-breaker is a result submitted after the normal deadline because it is new.
- Moderna presents the detailed INTerpath-001 data on Saturday 10/24. The plan in B-854 exits at the Friday 10/23 close and does not hold through the data. Results can move a stock far past any stop overnight. A stop is a standing order to sell at a set price, and a gap skips right over it. Moderna closed Friday at 218.00, down 7.8%.
- Merck has three first-time Phase 3 readouts in a top-billed session. Regeneron has a late-breaking survival result. Celcuity has a late-breaker on Saturday.
- ORIC presents first-line lung cancer data. I passed on a run-up trade there (B-966). It is four trading days away, under my five-day minimum, and the best plan risked about $1 to make $1.30. I want at least $1.50 for every $1 risked. I am still watching both levels: a close above 12.77 (MC-214) and a close below 11.31 (MC-215). It closed Friday at 12.35.

**Four more plans I passed on, and why it is the same reason.**
Eyepoint, Kymera, Syndax and Compass all have trial results due "in the fourth quarter" or "by year-end" (B-962 to B-965). A window is not a date. A trade into an event only works when you know which close to sell at before the news. With no date, the news can land any morning. All four are passes.

**What would change my mind about this week:** a Meta capex cut where the suppliers still close Wednesday's session green. That would tell me the market had already priced the cut in. I have no prediction logged on Meta's number itself, and I am not making one here.
