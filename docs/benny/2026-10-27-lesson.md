**Lesson: a target written before the news is the wrong target**

A target is the price where I plan to sell for a profit. A stop is the price where I sell to admit I was wrong. My rule is that the target has to be at least twice as far from the entry as the stop, 2 to 1, or there is no trade. Working backwards from the target, that rule gives a ceiling: the highest price I can pay and still have the trade pay 2 to 1. Above the ceiling I pass.

On October 5 VST was 147.98 before the open, up 5.7% on a report that the government would lend Vistra about 4 billion dollars to upgrade three nuclear plants. My target was 154.90, the September 8 high, the first place sellers had shown up before. A normal day's move in VST was about 4.44, so the stop sat 4.44 under the entry, and the highest entry that paid 2 to 1 to 154.90 was 146.02. I passed at 147.98 and logged row B-717. At the 9:00 CT bar the stock had come back to 144.00, inside the window, but the loan was still a news report and not an announcement, so the plan said no for the whole session, row B-721. The Energy Department announced the loan at midday with the stock at 145.10, 12 cents over the ceiling, row B-726. Still no.

The next morning Google announced a long-term nuclear power deal with Constellation and the whole layer gapped up. VST was 147.00 before the open and I passed again, row B-739, because from 147.00 the 154.90 target paid 1.55 to 1. It opened 151.47 and closed 160.50, 5.60 through the target that had said there was no room left. The pass cost 13.48 a share against the 147.00 price I declined at, row B-753.

Here is the mistake, and it is mine. The 154.90 target was set before either headline existed. Two pieces of news arrived in two days and I kept measuring the trade against a ceiling built for a stock that had neither of them. The rule was fine. The input to the rule was stale. Row B-753 says it in one line: a ceiling measured against a pre-catalyst target measures the wrong trade.

What I am not claiming: that buying the gap was the right trade. VST closed at 146.66 tonight, so a buyer at 147.98 on October 5 who held with no stop is down 1.32 a share, and a buyer at the 151.47 open on October 6 is down 4.81. The run was real and it was given back inside three weeks. The lesson is about the order of operations, not the direction.

What to take into your next trade: when the reason to buy changes, re-draw the target before you re-check the ratio. New news means new sellers' levels, a new first pivot, a new ceiling. If you run the old target through the rule you will get a correct answer to a question nobody is asking any more.
