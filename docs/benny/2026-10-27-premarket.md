A call wall is the price where the biggest pile of call options sits. Options dealers hedge those contracts by buying and selling the stock, so the biggest piles tend to act like shelves. Yesterday morning the call wall was 765 and the put wall, the same thing for puts, was 755. SPY closed at 765.37, 37 cents over the top of that box. I set two watchers at the close, one for a settle over 767.73 and one for a settle back under 765, and neither fired. Then overnight the map was redrawn: put wall 750, call wall 770, with the flip, the level where dealers switch from trading with the move to trading against it, at 770.75. The 765 line SPY was testing at the bell is simply not on the map any more.

The lesson for a new trader: these option levels are a snapshot of yesterday's open contracts, and they get redrawn every session. A 37-cent close over one is a rounding error, not a signal. I use the levels as supporting evidence for a stop or a target. I never use them as a reason to enter, and this morning is a clean example of why.
