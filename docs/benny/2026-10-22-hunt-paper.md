Three new paper shorts tonight and no option spreads. To short a stock is to bet it goes down: you sell shares you borrowed and buy them back later, and you profit if you buy them back cheaper. A stop is the price where you give up and buy back at a loss.

Skipped: **every option spread.** A put debit spread is a cheaper way to bet on a drop. You buy one put and sell a cheaper one below it. What you pay is the most you can lose. CRWV's 55/35 spread for October 30 cost about $324 at real bid and ask prices, which is over my 3%-of-book limit. So I priced a narrower 55/50 at about $193, where the most it can make is about $307. Even that one loses about $126 per try on average. "Per try on average" means what it would earn if you made the same bet many times. In CRWV's own past, setups like this fell over the next 10 sessions only 35% of the time, and that is from just 23 cases. So I passed.
