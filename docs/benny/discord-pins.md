===== #how-benny-works (message 1) =====
**How Benny works**
Benny is a research bot. He never places a real order. He runs a paper account that starts at $10,000 of pretend money, and he writes down every call before the outcome is known.

**What a trade plan says**
- **Ticker and direction:** what he would buy (long) or bet against (short).
- **Entry:** the price range where he would get in. Outside it, he waits.
- **Stop:** the price where he gets out to cap the loss. It is set before the trade, not during it.
- **Target:** where he takes profit. Usually he sells half at the first target.
- **Reward-to-risk:** how far the target is compared with the stop. He wants at least 2:1, meaning the possible gain is twice the possible loss.
- **Size:** how many shares, worked out from how much he is willing to lose if the stop hits.
- **Exit date:** the latest day he holds before getting out anyway.
- **The reason:** why this trade, in one or two lines.

If any of those are missing, it is not a plan.

===== #how-benny-works (message 2) =====
**What the labels mean**
- **ENTER:** the setup is live and the price is in range. The paper trade goes on.
- **NO TRADE:** the setup failed a check. The reason is always given.
- **WATCH:** close, but not ready. He names the price or event that would change his mind.
- **Grade (A to F):** how good the setup is. **Readiness (0 to 100):** how ready it is right now. Under 50 means watch only.

**How calls get graded**
Every call gets a decide-by date. When it comes, the call is scored against what the price actually did:
- **Hit:** reached the target before the stop.
- **Miss:** hit the stop first.
- **Timeout:** neither, by the decide-by date.

Misses get posted as loudly as wins. That's in #scorecard every week.

Around 9:00: the entry check. That's when paper trades go on.
After the close: the closing read, the paper book, the evening hunt.
Nightly: one lesson. Weekly: the scorecard.

===== #glossary (message 1) =====
**Glossary: orders and basics**
**Long:** you buy, hoping the price goes up.
**Short:** you bet the price goes down, by selling borrowed shares or buying puts.
**Paper trade:** a practice trade with pretend money, tracked like a real one.
**Market order:** buy or sell right now at whatever price is there. Fast, but the price can surprise you.
**Limit order:** buy or sell only at your price or better. It may not fill.
**Day order:** an order that cancels itself at the close if it has not filled.
**Bid / ask:** the best price buyers are paying and the best price sellers are asking. The gap between them is the **spread**.
**Volume:** how many shares traded. A move on heavy volume means more than a move on light volume.
**Pre-market / after-hours:** trading before 8:30 or after 3:00 Central. Thinner and jumpier than the regular session.
**Gap up / gap down:** the stock opens well above or below the last close.

===== #glossary (message 2) =====
**Glossary: risk and sizing**
**Stop-loss (stop):** the price where you sell to cap the loss. Decide it before you buy.
**Target:** the price where you take profit.
**Reward-to-risk (R:R):** the target distance divided by the stop distance. 2:1 means you stand to make twice what you could lose.
**R:** one unit of risk. If your stop risks $50, then +2R is a $100 gain and -1R is a $50 loss.
**Position size:** how many shares. Work it out from the dollars you will risk: risk ÷ (entry - stop) = shares.
**Risk band:** the most of the account one trade is allowed to risk.
**ATR (Average True Range):** how far the stock normally moves in a day. A stop closer than one ATR usually gets hit by ordinary wiggle.
**Drawdown:** how far the account has fallen from its high.
**Invalidation:** the price or event that proves the idea wrong. Get out when it happens, no arguing.

===== #glossary (message 3) =====
**Glossary: reading the chart**
**Support:** a price where buyers have stepped in before. **Resistance:** a price where sellers have.
**Pivot:** a turning point on the chart, a recent high or low the price reacted to.
**Breakout:** the price closes above resistance, ideally on heavy volume. **Breakdown:** closes below support.
**Moving average (50-day, 200-day):** the average closing price over that many days. Above the 200-day is generally a healthy trend. Below it, be careful.
**Relative strength:** how a stock is doing compared with the market (SPY, QQQ) or its sector. Leaders outperform.
**Buy zone:** the price range where a plan says to enter. Above it, you are chasing.
**Extended:** the price has run far above its averages. Higher odds of a pullback.
**Regime (risk-on / risk-off):** the market's overall mood. Risk-off means fewer and smaller trades.
**VIX:** the market's fear gauge. It rises when traders expect big swings.

===== #glossary (message 4) =====
**Glossary: options and catalysts**
**Call:** a contract that gains when the stock rises. **Put:** gains when it falls.
**Strike:** the price the option is built around. **Expiry:** the date it ends.
**Premium:** what the option costs. Buying one, the most you can lose is the premium.
**Debit spread:** buy one option and sell another further out. It's cheaper and the loss is capped, but so is the gain.
**Defined risk:** a trade where the most you can lose is known up front.
**Leveraged ETF (2x, 3x):** a fund that moves two or three times the daily move. It resets every day, so holding it for weeks can drift away from 2x.
**Catalyst:** a dated event that can move a stock: earnings, an FDA decision, a product launch.
**Earnings:** the quarterly report. Stocks can gap hard either way. Benny never holds an options trade through one.
**PDUFA date:** the deadline for an FDA decision on a drug.
**Guidance:** what the company says about the next quarter. It often matters more than the results.
