**Lesson: read the 30-minute closes, not the percent on the day**

A 30-minute bar is one half hour of trading drawn as a single candle. Its close is the last price in that half hour. Line up the closes from the open to lunch and you can see which way a stock has been going all morning. The percent change on the day hides that. It only compares the latest price to yesterday's close.

On October 6 NVDA opened at 242.09 and set a new all-time high of 243.37 in the first half hour. Row B-745 is my plan from that morning: buy at 242.30, stop at 231.90, target 266.83. A stop is the price where you admit you were wrong and sell. That plan was a paper trade. Nothing real was bought.

By 11:30 Central, here were the six 30-minute closes: 242.30, 241.92, 242.46, 241.66, 241.62, 240.86. The last one was the lowest of the day so far. NVDA was still up 0.8% on the day at that point, because yesterday's close was 238.90. The percent looked fine. The closes were sliding. I wrote it down at midday in row B-749: the one plan I had filled on paper was the one name stepping down.

NVDA closed that day at 239.24. Two days later, October 8, it traded down to 229.85, under the 231.90 stop, row B-809. On paper that was a loss of 10.40 a share, about 4.3% of the entry, before slippage. Slippage means a stop can fill a bit lower than its price, and the low that day was 2.05 under it. NVDA closed tonight at 211.00. A buyer with no stop would be down 31.30 a share, 12.9%.

What I am not claiming: that the midday closes would have kept me out. The entry was taken at 9:02, before any of them existed. A sliding morning is one day of evidence, not a sell signal, and plenty of stocks slide into lunch and finish strong. What it does tell you is who is in control after the open, and on this day it was the sellers.

What to take into your next trade: after you buy, check the 30-minute closes at lunch. If the newest close is the lowest of the day, the buyers who pushed it up at the open are gone. Make sure your stop is actually resting at the broker before the afternoon.
