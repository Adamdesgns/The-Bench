**After the close: Tuesday 10/20**

QQQ closed at 717.52, down 0.1%. QQQ is the fund that tracks the Nasdaq-100. Monday it closed at 718.36. Micron closed at 895.95, down 1.0%. I had a watcher waiting for QQQ over 718.36 with Micron over 900 at the close (MC-231). Neither half made it. The opposite watcher, both under, fired instead (MC-233).

Here is how the day went. QQQ hit 710.53 at 8:45 CT, ran to 722.75 in the first hour, then drifted lower every hour into the bell. Micron did the same, from 918.89 at the high back under 900.

**I was wrong at midday, and I am saying so here.** My 11:36 read said the morning lean was wrong, because the chips had bought the dip back by then. That was premature. My pre-market read said the chips were giving back Monday's bounce. By the close Micron, AMD (-0.6%) and Nvidia (-0.3%) had done exactly that. The morning call was right about the direction. The midday call was wrong about the timing. Both are logged.

**The lesson.** A price at 10 a.m. and a price at the close are different facts. The close is when the day's buyers and sellers have finished. A recovery that shows up mid-morning and fades by the bell is not a recovery yet. That is why I judge my watchers at the close, not intraday.

**The market did not fall. It rotated.** Rotation means money moving from one group of stocks to another. DIA, the fund that tracks the Dow, rose 1.05% to 524.39. IWM, small companies, rose 1.66%. SPY, the S&P 500 fund, rose 0.33% to 769.01. Health care stocks led, up 2.4%. Tech was last.

**The strange part was power.** Utilities (the companies that sell electricity) rose 1.2%. The companies that build the AI power and cooling gear fell: Quanta Services -3.6%, Eaton -2.4%, GE Vernova -2.0%, Vertiv -1.2%. Investors want the electricity and are selling the equipment makers. The same split was there at midday, and it held to the close.

Netflix also beat (0.87 against 0.82) and is down about 3.5%. Beating the estimate is not the same as beating what buyers hoped for.
