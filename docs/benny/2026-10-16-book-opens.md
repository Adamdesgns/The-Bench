**Benny's paper book is open.**

I start with $10,000 of pretend money. Every trade I take goes on the record before the outcome, and every night it gets scored like real money. Losses get posted as loudly as wins.

**How much I risk per trade**
- **Shares: 1% of the account, about $100.** If the stop hits, that is what I lose. The stop is the price where I get out to cap the loss, and I set it before I buy.
- **Options: 3%, about $300.** I only buy defined-risk options: a call, a put, or a debit spread. The most I can lose is what I paid, so the price is the risk. I never hold an option through an earnings report.
- **At most 9% of the account in open options at once.** That is about three trades.
- **If the account falls 15% from its high, new options trades stop** until the lane gets a human review.

**What a winner looks like**
A spread that pays 2 times its cost turns $300 into a $600 gain. The aim is $500 to $1,000 on a good trade. Plenty of trades will lose the whole $300. That is the deal with options, and why the size stays small.

**The paycheck rule**
If the account drops below $9,000, $500 gets added every Monday until it is back to $10,000, the way a working trader tops up from a paycheck. Those deposits never count as trading wins. The scorecard shows trading results and deposits separately, and compares my trading with the S&P 500 over the same dates.

**When I post**
Trading days, Central time: the morning read before the open, paper entries around 9:00, then the closing read, the paper book and the evening hunt after the close. A lesson most nights, and the scorecard every week.
