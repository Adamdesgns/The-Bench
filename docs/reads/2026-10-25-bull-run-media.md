# The 2026 bull run: how the biggest runners reached the public

**Private read, 2026-10-25 (Sunday).** Prices re-pulled from Robinhood tonight; the official settle used is Friday 2026-10-23. This is data on how the names were pushed into public view. It is not a call on any of them.

## The short version

- **I found no paid pump behind any of the 11 parabolic names.** No "sponsored" or "paid advertisement" disclosure on any page read, and no SEC or FINRA action, trading halt or named paid promoter. That is "not found" from web and EDGAR searches, not proof none exists. Reddit and X could not be read directly.
- **The odd media signal in this run wasn't paid promotion. It was timing.** Retail-facing outlets (Motley Fool, InvestorPlace, 24/7 Wall St, Fool Canada) clustered their "up X%" and "smart money is loading up" pieces at the top or just after it: 6 of the 9 parabolic names with retail coverage had one within 5 sessions of the high. Mainstream outlets and analyst notes were there from the start, and retail sentiment did not lead the moves.
- **The real red flag was companies selling stock into the run.** AAOI filed a $600M at-the-market stock sale (shares sold into the market over time) the morning after its all-time high. KEEL sold $458M of convertible notes about two weeks before its peak. Both were 29-50% lower 40 trading days later. IonQ ($2B) and Nebius ($5.75B convertible) raised later, into the decline. None of the four steady runners sold stock; their filings were bond deals.
- **The micro-caps looked different.** Templated alert sites covered them (StocksToTrade, which is Tim Sykes' network; Schaeffer's; Tickeron). Two of them ran while sitting on exchange notices for trading under $1 (QSI's run cured its notice; SES has filed for a reverse split). An insider sold PacBio at the high. SES rose 36% on 80.7M shares on a day with no news.

## 5. Did the parabolic names carry these signals more than the steady ones?

- **Yes for company stock sales.** 2 of 11 parabolic names sold stock or convertibles within about ten sessions of the high (AAOI, KEEL), IRD had a $125M program ready, and 3 more raised into the decline (NBIS, AAOI again, IONQ). The steady group: 0 of 4 (bond deals only).
- **Yes for retail-promo timing.** 6 of the 9 parabolic names with retail coverage had a gain-framed piece within 5 sessions of the high (MU, NBIS, KEEL, IONQ, PACB, QSI). SNDK, BE and AAOI did not; their retail pieces came mid-run or weeks after. The steady names drew mainstream earnings coverage that moved with price.
- **Not much for analyst target chases.** 5 of 11 parabolic names, but also 2 of 4 steady ones. It doesn't separate the groups.
- **Yes, and only in the micro-caps, for alert-site coverage and exchange notices.** Four of four micro/small autumn runners showed at least one: alert sites, an under-$1 notice, a no-news spike, or an insider sale at the top.
- **No for insider selling.** Insiders sold into every name, steady ones included (NVDA's director sold $298M; AVGO's Samueli about $350M). Insider selling alone doesn't separate a parabolic name from a steady one in this run. KEEL's insiders bought after the crash, the only real buying found.
- **No for paid pumps.** Zero disclosed paid promotions on any of the 15 names. Paid promotion showed up only as Benzinga "17(b)" ads for other small-cap issuers next to a BE article.

## 6. What this suggests (observation, not a call)

1. **"Pushed into the public" in this run meant the company and the analysts, not paid promoters.** For mid and large caps the push came from company releases, analyst targets, index inclusion (NBIS into the Nasdaq-100), fund 13Fs (Situational Awareness) and leveraged ETFs (MUU past $5B; SNXX +640% per one source, unverified). Retail-promo coverage was a lagging indicator.
2. **The most useful "media" tell was the company's own financing calendar.** A prospectus or convertible filed while the stock is up 100%+ is the company saying the price is good enough to sell. On the two cases inside ten sessions of a high, the stock was 29-50% lower 40 sessions later (logged as a proposed pattern below; two cases is a story, not evidence).
3. **Gain-framed "up X%" or "loading up" headlines dated within 5 sessions of a high showed up on 6 of the 9 parabolic names where a retail piece was found.** SNDK, BE and AAOI are the misses; SNDK's Fool piece came mid-run, with most of the run still ahead. Logged as a pattern with all three misses counted.
4. **For micro-caps, the tells are structural:** an under-$1 notice that a run happens to cure, a reverse-split proxy right after, a no-news volume day, an at-the-market program in place before a data release, and templated alert-site posts. PACB is the one case where an alert site's coverage came before the spike. It is one instance.
5. **Retail sentiment was not a leading signal.** BE's Stocktwits mood was "extremely bearish" the day before its record; SNDK's Reddit activity was low at +569%.

**Gaps:** Reddit, X and YouTube data could not be read directly, so social-wave dates are mostly second-hand. The SEC litigation and FINRA databases were not queried directly. Several rows come from search summaries only (marked u in the source files). AAOI's insider and ATM share counts come partly from a weak single source (Parameter.io); the prospectus amounts and dates above come from EDGAR directly.

- **P-067 (proposed, 2/2):** a stock or convertible sale within 10 sessions of a 100%+ run's high marks the area of the top, and the stock is lower 40 sessions later. AAOI -38.4% (5/14 → 7/14), KEEL -29.2% (6/4 → 8/3). Two cases; it needs a third before it means anything.
- **P-068 (6/9 hits; the script labels it "supported"):** a gain-framed retail-promo piece lands within 5 sessions of a 100%+ run's high. The misses are SNDK, BE and AAOI. The chance-level comparison its test asks for (how much of each run falls inside the 11-session window) is not computed yet, so treat 6/9 as raw.
