# FED EVIDENCE - Wednesday 2026-09-16 09:35 CT (decision 13:00 CT, dot plot and Warsh press conference after)

Adam: "Gather evidence on whether the fed will hike rates or not." This is evidence, not a call. Current target range 3.50-3.75 percent, unchanged since 2023; a hike takes it to 3.75-4.00, the first increase since July 2023.

## WHAT THE MARKETS SAY (money on the line)

- Fed funds futures, CME FedWatch: 92.5 to 93 percent for a quarter-point hike (Benzinga, Tuesday 08:56 and Wednesday 05:35 CT). Path: 84 percent on 9/14, 86 to 90 after the 9/11 CPI, roughly 70 the day before, 64 on 9/2, 57 in late August.
- Central Bank Watch, as of 9/15: 91 percent higher, 9 hold, 0 cut. Beyond today the curve prices 56 basis points of tightening over three meetings, about 2.2 hikes: October 43 percent, December 88 percent cumulative, rate 4.18 by December.
- Polymarket: 88 to 89 percent for 25 basis points, 11 percent no change, 164 to 193 million dollars traded. Three weeks ago, right after Warsh's Jackson Hole speech, the same contract was 49 cents.
- Kalshi: 58 percent on 9/2, 26 cents on 8/15; no fresh print pulled this morning. Kalshi's December contract puts 39 percent on a full half point by year-end and 16 percent on no change at all.
- The gap that existed in early September (futures 64, Kalshi 58, Polymarket 56) has closed; every market is now above 88.

## WHAT THE DATA SAYS (why they moved)

- August CPI (9/11): headline plus 0.4 on the month, 3.4 percent year over year; core plus 0.3 on the month, 2.4 percent annual. Energy plus 2.1 on the month, gasoline plus 3.9, energy up 16.3 percent over twelve months. Airline fares plus 23.4 percent on the year, shelter plus 3.0. Strategists are calling it "stickier inflation," not a one-time shock.
- August PPI (9/10): plus 0.4 on the month, 5.4 percent year over year.
- August payrolls: plus 162,000, up from 21,000 in July.
- August retail sales (this morning): plus 1.2 percent against 0.8 expected, after a revised minus 0.5 in July. Import prices plus 0.7 against 0.5 expected.
- Soft spots this week: Empire State 7.6 against 15 expected (from 20.6), New York Fed services minus 8.7 against minus 2.2, mortgage applications minus 4.1 percent with 30-year rates at their highest since May 2025.
- Rates and oil: the 10-year at 5.00 to 5.04 percent, highest since 2007; the 2-year 4.66; Brent 107 to 108, WTI about 104, with the Iran war ongoing. A CBO estimate cited by Senator Warren attributes nearly half of US inflation to the conflict.

## WHAT THE FED HAS SAID AND DONE

- July meeting: held at 3.50-3.75 for a fifth straight meeting, but three FOMC members dissented in favor of a hike. That is an unusual number of hawkish dissents and the strongest single piece of evidence that the committee was already leaning this way before the August data.
- Chair Kevin Warsh at Jackson Hole (late August): if the Fed is not confident underlying inflation is declining it has "work to do." Markets read it as hawkish; the hike contract went from a coin flip to near-certain over the following three weeks as the data confirmed it.
- The June dot plot median already had rates at 3.75-4.00 by December. A quarter-point today completes that forecast rather than exceeding it.
- Political pressure the other way: President Trump has said publicly the US should have lower rates.

## WHAT ECONOMISTS SAY (the case for a hold)

- Reuters poll, 9/4 to 9/9, before the CPI: 65 of 93 economists expected a hold, though the share expecting at least one 2026 hike more than doubled from the prior month. That poll is the strongest hold evidence and it is a week stale.
- Goldman Sachs called a September hike "very unlikely" and expects a hold through 2026. J.P. Morgan Wealth Management expects 25 basis points. MNI's compilation has a median of 50 basis points of hikes this cycle; forecasts run from zero to three. Bank of America sees 75 basis points total, delivered faster, by end-2026. 22V Research: hike today, one more penciled in for 2026.
- The bear-for-stocks angle: JPMorgan estimates the S&P 500 falls 1.25 to 1.75 percent on a surprise hold because it would read as the Fed not defending its inflation credibility; a hike framed as one-and-done could send yields lower and stocks higher (Gary Black's argument).

## THE BALANCE

For a hike: every money market above 88 percent and still rising, three dissents already on record in July, a chair who set the bar at "confident inflation is declining" and then got a 3.4 percent CPI, a 5.4 percent PPI, 162,000 jobs and a 1.2 percent retail sales print. Against: the economists' poll (stale), Goldman, the soft regional surveys this week, mortgage demand cracking, and a president who wants cuts. On the record as of 09:35 CT: the weight of evidence is a hike, and the decision that actually moves the tape is the dot plot one minute later, whether it shows one more hike in 2026 or a campaign.
