# TRENDING-SNAPSHOT read - 2026-10-02 13:11 CT (data pulled 13:11 CT)

STX is 838.61, down 11.31% from 945.57. WDC is 416.09, down 10.04% from 462.56. Together they have lost about 41B of market value today. The headline behind it is a 380M spend. The one thing to watch is whether STX holds the bounce off its 791.75 low into the close.

The catalyst is a Nikkei report, carried in CNBC's midday movers, that Toshiba will double its production capacity for hard disk drives used in data centres and spend about 380M to expand plants in the Philippines. The volume is real. STX has traded 10.68M shares against a 30-day average of 3.72M for a whole day, which is 2.87x. WDC has traded 18.39M against 6.66M, which is 2.76x.

The lesson is in the size of the reaction. STX lost about 24.3B and WDC about 16.8B on a capex number about a hundredth of their combined loss. The market is not pricing Toshiba's spend. It is pricing the end of a shortage. Both stocks ran this year because data-centre drives were scarce and the makers could set the price. A shortage stock is priced on the shortage, and news of new supply hits the price story even when demand has not changed. The rest of the stack agrees that demand is fine: SMH is up 2.02% and DELL, which sells servers and storage, is up 4.24%. MU, the other scarcity name, is down 1.90% (1,076.52) on a green chip day, so some of the same doubt has reached memory.

On the tape, STX has bounced 46.86 off its low and sits 90% of the way up the day's range, so buyers met the flush. WDC is only 62% up its range. Neither name is on the board. They sit at the storage seam of the box layer.

The call is no trade on both, logged as B-697 (STX) and B-698 (WDC). Buying a falling knife on day one of a supply story is guessing. The reclaim line is now on the mind-changer list as MC-036, expiring 10/16.

What would change my mind: STX closing back over 945.57, yesterday's close. That would mean the market decided the Toshiba capacity does not end the shortage.
