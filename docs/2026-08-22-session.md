## 2. THE FIVE THINGS WORTH REMEMBERING

**1. `benny-v1.md` was written 2026-08-14 and no routine had ever read it.** Benny published daily for eight days with his persona file sitting unread on disk. The general form — **a prompt nothing points at does nothing** — also explains marquee, which was being cited in conversation while orphaned in the file system. *Check the wiring before writing a new file.*

**2. `LTH` was on the AI board and it is a gym.** Life Time Group Holdings, Consumer Services, 44,000 employees. It survived because the board was prose duplicated across five routine files, and a ticker list buried in prose is a list nobody audits. It is now `db/board.json`, one place, 22 tickers each verified against `get_equity_fundamentals`.

**3. The six-part brick had a mechanical cause, not a stylistic one.** 24 of the last 25 posts were exactly 6 parts. Four routines said *"Target 400-700 words"* and *"write a LONG-FORM X Article"* while the chain rules in the same file said long-form was retired and every part must be under 280 characters. Told to write long **and** in short parts, the routines wrote the longest thing allowed. **Both instructions were being followed, which is why nobody caught it.**

**4. Our stop rule is tighter than the convention, and our own book proves it.** v25's ATR Floor is **1.0 ATR**. The common convention is **1.5–3×, most often 2–3×**. GDS was stopped at 1.064 ATR — *above* our floor — and closed **0.74 above that stop the same afternoon**. HPQ, stopped at 0.11 ATR, did the same thing two days earlier. Two consecutive stop-outs reversing inside the day they fired, plus P-013 (all three closed swings exited at their stop, none reached T1). **B-156 reopened the audit.** On GDS a 2× stop sits near 31.02; the session low was 32.385 and never came close. The floor cost **$11.86** on that one trade. *No framework change made — this is evidence, and the call is Adam's.*

**5. The audience was wrong for months, and the operator proved it.** The inherited definition came from the retired article prompt: *"active traders who recognise weak analysis immediately."* Writing for that reader produces posts that are correct and teach nobody. A stops explainer opened with *"I put our stop 1.06 ATR under the entry"* and **Adam could not follow it.** If it loses the operator it loses everyone. The audience is now **new followers and new traders who want to learn and grow an account safely**, and the fix that made the post work was leading in dollars — *"$1.80 below the buy, on a stock that moves $1.69 a day"* — with ATR arriving afterward as the *name* for something already understood.
