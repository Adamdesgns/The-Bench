The problem is that none of that is what the stock is trading on. Two weeks ago the company named a new CEO, and the market disliked the choice enough to knock 10% off in a single day; the stock has fallen every week since and is now 41% below its July high. Before that, the people who run the company sold $11.3 million of their own stock near the top and bought none. The business is getting better and the ownership is getting worse, and right now the ownership is winning. There is a line worth watching — the 200-day average around $78, about 2.7% below here — because that is where the multi-year uptrend either holds or ends. Until the stock proves something above $85, the honest answer is that cash is the better position, and a falling price is not the same thing as a cheap one.
