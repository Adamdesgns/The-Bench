## 1. What Hims & Hers actually is

A subscription telehealth platform. You answer an intake form, a licensed provider reviews it, a prescription is filled by Hims' own pharmacies and mailed on a recurring bill. The numbers, Q2 2026:

| | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Subscribers | 2.89M (+19%) | 2.44M |
| Monthly revenue per subscriber | $92 (+21%) | $76 |
| Revenue | $753.2M (+38%) | $544.8M |
| of which US | $621.8M (+16%) | $537.3M |
| of which rest of world | $131.4M (Eucalyptus, closed June) | $7.5M |
| Gross margin | **64%** | **76%** |
| Net income | **−$86.3M** | +$42.5M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $60.3M | $82.2M |
| Free cash flow | **−$68.2M** | −$69.4M |

2026 guide raised to $3.1–3.3B revenue and $275–325M adjusted EBITDA. Q3 guide $880–900M. 2030 target $6.5B revenue and $1.3B adjusted EBITDA.

**Categories:** weight loss (the growth engine and the margin problem), sexual health, hair, skin, mental health, **testosterone** (management: the fastest-growing category outside weight loss, on track to be the sixth US specialty at a $100M run rate), and lab testing. International is Eucalyptus (UK, Australia, Germany, Japan, Canada), rebranded Pilot → Hims in Australia on 8/31.

## 2. Do they produce GLP-1s? Not any more, not in any way that matters.

The timeline, from the 10-Q and the primary reporting:

- **May 2024:** started selling **compounded semaglutide** during the FDA shortage, through 503A compounding pharmacies (never their own 503B for GLP-1s).
- **Feb 5, 2026:** launched a **$49/month compounded copy of Novo's new Wegovy pill**. Withdrawn within days under FDA and HHS pressure. Novo sued for patent infringement in Delaware; the '343 patent runs to **December 5, 2031**.
- **March 2026, the "2026 US WL Announcement":** pivot to **branded** GLP-1s. Hims now sells Novo's Wegovy pen and pill at Novo's cash price, **$149/month**, keeps compounded GLP-1s "on a limited scale," and no longer advertises compounded. Restructuring charges of **$38.1M** in the first half.
- **March 9, 2026:** Novo dismissed its suit **without prejudice**, reserving the right to refile.
- **May 2026:** generic semaglutide in **Canada**, where Novo's patent lapsed in January. A third-party generic, not Hims-made.

So today Hims is a **distributor of Novo's drug at Novo's price**. That is the whole gross-margin story: 76% a year ago, 64% now, and the 10-Q says the mix shift "is expected to persist." Morgan Stanley models 64% through 2027. Revenue up 38%, gross profit up 15%.

**What they own:** 503A pharmacies in Ohio and Arizona, a 503B sterile compounding facility and a **peptide manufacturing facility** in California (acquired Feb 2025), lab testing operations, and in **July 2026 a deal worth up to $30M** to acquire the expertise and technology to manufacture peptide active ingredients themselves.

## 3. What they are working on next: peptides

This is the bet management keeps pointing at. On **July 23, 2026** FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted **8–6 (one abstention)** to recommend adding **BPC-157**, plus KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, epitalon and Semax, to the 503A bulk-substances list. That is a recommendation, not a rule. FDA had removed BPC-157 from Category 2 in April without putting it on Category 1, so it sits in a gray zone, and formal rulemaking realistically runs into **2027**.

Management on the Q2 call: stability and validation testing on BPC-157 at Menlo Park, plans to offer **sermorelin, glutathione and NAD+ by year-end**, and "move extremely quickly if the FDA moves these peptides to the Category 1 list." The July know-how deal is the supply chain for that.

Also live: the new Hers app with AI-led care (patients message care teams 3× as much, non-clinical human tasks down 50%), a Hers smart scale, testosterone expansion into "five or six overseas markets," and the Wegovy pill launch that management called "staggering."

## 4. The lawsuit, exactly

**FTC v. Hims & Hers**, filed **July 29, 2026** in the Northern District of California, joined by Utah and Los Angeles County for California. Section 5 of the FTC Act plus ROSCA plus state law. Three allegations:

1. **Health data sharing.** Sensitive conditions and personal health data sent to Meta and Snap through tracking pixels and customer-list uploads, while marketing promised privacy.
2. **Bill-on-intake.** Customers enrolled in recurring prescription plans the moment they submitted the intake form, before any provider consult, without genuine consent.
3. **Buried cancellation.** Phone/email/chat only before 2023; the online button buried afterward.

Remedies sought: permanent injunction, **monetary relief for an unspecified amount**, civil penalties. Background: a Civil Investigative Demand since **October 2023**, three years of cooperation, settlement talks that failed. Management on the call: "not prepared to accept terms it believes do not reflect the facts or the law."

**What the 10-Q booked:** a **legal contingency accrual of approximately $60 million** as of 6/30 under ASC 450, with the statement that the company "is unable to reasonably predict the outcome... or to estimate a reasonably possible financial loss or range of financial loss in excess of the amount accrued." The Q2 GAAP loss included $81M of non-recurring costs; the accrual is inside that.

**Attached to it:** a securities class action, *Velanki v. Hims & Hers*, 26-cv-09313, class period **8/4/2025 to 7/29/2026**, lead-plaintiff deadline **11/2/2026**. And separately, Visa placed the weight-loss subscription business in its dispute-monitoring program in August (Bloomberg): same billing complaints, different enforcer.
