## THE WHY

The list was built on a screen that mostly is not true — two-thirds of these names are well off their lows, and a few are having good days. Of the eleven genuinely near a 52-week low, four set that low in the last week, which means nobody knows where the bottom is yet and there is nothing to define risk against. That leaves seven, and among those exactly one has the thing you actually want to see: Nike's chief executive and three of its directors each bought stock with their own money in April, roughly $2.7 million between them, and the stock is now cheaper than every price they paid. Behind that buying, gross margin has improved nearly nine points in a year and profit is five times what it was. Revenue still has not recovered, so this is early rather than obvious. The second name, VICI, is duller and safer — a casino landlord collecting contractual rent, priced at book value with a 6.3% dividend. Everything else on the list is either fine but being sold by its own executives, or too fresh off a low to be anything other than a guess.
