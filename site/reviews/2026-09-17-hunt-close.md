# Hunt at the close — Thursday 2026-09-17

**Fifty-seven names scanned. Zero fired. Three cleared the band, and all three lost money on our own arithmetic.**

That is the whole report, and it is a real result rather than an empty one. The screens looked at 57 tickers on today's settled closes, from both sides — breakouts on the long side, breakdowns on the short side. Not one cleared the confirmation gate, which needs a name to actually close at or under its 20-session low on at least one and a half times its normal volume. Twenty-one got close enough to keep watching. Of those, only seven had an option spread I could price inside seven to fourteen days, and only three of those spreads cost less than the $302 risk band. Every one of those three carried negative expected value — meaning if you took that exact trade over and over, the history of that stock says you end up behind.

For scale, the account is $3,018.64, so the band — the most I will ever put at risk on one idea — is $301.86. Buying power is $62.41, which binds hard here: even the cheapest of the three spreads costs $55.50 and the other two are beyond reach in cash terms. I am not ranking on that, only reporting it.

## Advance Auto Parts (AAP) — the only real catalyst, and still a no

AAP closed at $41.96, well under its 50-day average of $50.81 and within a dollar of its 20-session low. It is the weakest name on the board against the S&P over the last month, down 24% relative. What it did not do is break down on volume — today's trade was 0.57x normal, so the gate never fired.

The spread I priced: buy the $42 put, sell the $37.50 put, both expiring September 25th, eight days out. That costs $1.125 per share, or **$112.50 for one contract — and that $112.50 is the most you can lose, no matter what happens.** The most you can make is $337.50, which happens only if AAP is at or below $37.50 on expiration day. You start making money below $40.88.

Now the odds. Looking at every time AAP has set up this way in its own history — 75 instances, which is enough to take seriously, so I grade this evidence an A — the stock went the right direction 44% of the time over the next ten sessions, but only reached this far down 13.3% of the time. Robinhood's own screen puts the chance of profit at 35.7%, so our directional read is about eight points better than the market's. That sounds encouraging until you finish the multiplication: 13.3% of $337.50 is about $45, and the 56% chance of losing the $112.50 costs about $63. **Net expected value: minus $18 per contract, or minus $3 for every day you hold it.**

The catalyst is genuinely live, and it is the best one on the board. UBS said on Monday that aftermarket demand has been soft and that AutoZone's quarterly comparable sales will likely miss what Wall Street expects — 2.5% against a 3.6% consensus. AutoZone reports September 22nd, which falls inside this expiration. AAP's own August 20th quarter showed comparable sales falling 0.5% on weak do-it-yourself demand, and three brokers cut their price targets on it (RBC $67 to $52, UBS $65 to $47, DA Davidson $58 to $48). AAP does not report itself until October 29th, so there is no earnings landmine inside the trade. Insiders have done nothing but take routine grants.

Two things kill it anyway. The $37.50 put you would be selling quotes five cents bid against twenty cents asked, with one contract of open interest and eight traded today — there is effectively no market there, so you would be paying nearly the full price of the long put and getting almost nothing back for the short one. And the entry would land Friday, which is triple witching plus the S&P and Dow quarterly rebalance: the single most distorted session of the quarter, and a poor first day for a structure with only six sessions to live.

**Watch AutoZone's print on the 22nd. Do not pay for this structure.**

## Core Scientific (CORZ) — the catalyst gate fails outright

CORZ closed at $17.14, below its 50-day of $19.80. The screen liked the shape. The news does not support it at all: **Wells Fargo initiated coverage this morning at Overweight with a $28 price target**, and the average target across analysts is $35.63. A short position needs a live reason the selling continues, and an upgrade the same session is the opposite of that.

The insider record does not rescue it either. One insider, Duchene, has sold exactly 10,000 shares on five occasions — August 19th and 26th, September 2nd, 10th and 16th. Identical size on a regular cadence is the fingerprint of a pre-scheduled selling plan filed months in advance, not someone reacting to bad news. Meanwhile two directors bought shares on the open market in August, at $18.33 and $19.19, and both are underwater today.

There was one genuinely bearish footprint: someone paid $553,000 for 3,500 put contracts at the $13 strike expiring April 2027, against only 67 contracts of existing open interest. That is a real bet, but it is a seven-month hedge, not a reason for a six-day trade.

The spread would have been the $17 put against the $15 put for September 25th, costing $55.50 with $144.50 of upside. The odds from CORZ's own history are thin — 29 instances, graded B, direction right 41% of the time and reaching the target only 10% — giving minus $17.59 of expected value. And we already passed on this name yesterday (row B-426), when its 7% pop turned out to be sympathy from Cipher's grid announcement rather than anything about CORZ. It gave that back today, which means yesterday's call was right and the easy part of the move is gone.

**No trade.**

## Arteris (AIP) — ranked first, and the worst of the three

This was the top-ranked spread by expected value per day and it still lost money, which tells you how thin tonight was.

AIP closed at $22.36, down from a 50-day average of $27.07. The problem starts with the calendar: Arteris has no expiration between seven and fourteen days out — it goes from tomorrow straight to October 16th. So the only spread available is 21 trading sessions long, well outside the window this routine is built for.

Buy the $22.50 put, sell the $20 put, October 16th: **$135 to put on, and the most it can ever pay is $115.** That is the sentence that ends it. You are risking more than the structure can return, so even though our read on direction (46.6%) is more than eight points better than the market's 38.2%, the arithmetic comes out to minus $54.79.

And the price is not real. The $20 put you would sell is quoted ten cents bid against a dollar-fifteen asked, with one contract traded today. The $0.625 "mark" is just the midpoint of a market that is not there, so the true cost would be materially worse than $135 — which means the loss is not actually capped at the number on the screen.

The bearish case is genuine but stale. The CEO sold 100,000 shares for $3.05 million on August 3rd, a 10% holder sold twelve separate clips between $28 and $32, the CFO is retiring, and there have been zero open-market purchases against $7.78 million of sales. But the most recent filing is September 1st — sixteen days ago — and Roth started the stock at Buy with a $40 target on August 4th. Earnings are November 3rd, safely outside.

**No trade.**
