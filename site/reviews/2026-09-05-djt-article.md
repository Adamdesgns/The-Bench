# The $100,000 Subscription Nobody Is Pricing

### Trump Media quietly turned the President's social feed into a market-data product. The stock is still being traded like a political mood ring.

*The Bench · September 5, 2026 · All prices are Friday September 4 official closes.*

---

In August, a company with thirty-one employees started selling Wall Street a
subscription.

The product is called the Truth API. It costs **$60,000 to $100,000 a month**,
and what it delivers is latency — real-time, millisecond-grade access to posts
from prominent Truth Social accounts, the most prominent of which belongs to the
President of the United States. On the second-quarter call, Trump Media said it
had signed more than ten paying customers. CEO Kevin McGurn told critics the
criticism was "misinformed."

He has a point about the mechanics, at least. Every major platform sells a
firehose. Bloomberg sells latency. That is a normal business.

What is not normal is the input. On August 11, the President posted a demand
that Iran pay compensation for a five-month war. That post moved the pre-market
before a single wire carried it. Senator Elizabeth Warren's complaint is not that
Trump Media built an API. It is that the company is selling Wall Street early
access to **economic and national security information**, and that the man
generating the information owns the company selling it.

Set the ethics aside for sixty seconds, because there is a trading question
underneath and almost nobody is asking it.

**Trump Media is no longer a media company. It is two unrelated assets bolted
together — a levered bitcoin holding, and a tiny, legally contested market-data
startup — and the market is only pricing the first one.**

---

## What they actually built

Take the Truth API seriously for a moment, because it is genuinely strange.

There is no other public company whose core informational input is the unfiltered
output of a sitting head of state, owned by that head of state, sold by
subscription to funds that trade on it. It is not a media asset. It is closer to
an exchange data feed with a single, unpredictable, market-moving publisher.

If you were designing a business to monetize the exact thing that has been
whipsawing this tape all year — Iran, tariffs, the Fed, everything announced by
post first and confirmed by press release second — this is what it would look
like.

That is the bull case, and it is a real one.

## Then you do the arithmetic

Ten customers at the top of the stated range is **$100,000 a month each**. Call
it **$12 million a year**, and be generous.

Trump Media's market capitalization is **$2.504 billion**.

Twelve million against two and a half billion is **half of one percent.**

For scale on how small the operating business actually is: total company revenue
in the second quarter, every segment, was **$1.7 million**. Not per month. The
quarter.

They turned the President's thumbs into a market-data feed, and it still is not
half a percent of the company.

And the one line of the business that is genuinely novel is the one being sued.
In August, media groups filed suit seeking to shut down paid early access to
market-moving posts. Whatever you think of the merits, the legal risk is
concentrated precisely on the only asset here that nobody else has.

## So what is actually pricing the stock?

Bitcoin.

Trump Media holds roughly **$900 million** in bitcoin — **36% of the entire
market cap** — alongside a Truth.Fi segment built around crypto and crypto-linked
ETFs. It reported unrealized losses on that stash in the first half. On a GAAP
basis the company earns nothing; the trailing P/E is **-1.91**.

Strip it down and you are holding a bitcoin position with a social network
attached, a lawsuit in the mail, and a president-shaped sentiment factor on top.

Which brings us to the most useful number of the week, and the one that took
thirty seconds to check.

**On Friday, the iShares Bitcoin Trust fell 2.44%. DJT fell 5.36%.**

The stock dropped **more than twice as hard as its own bitcoin proxy** on a day
when bitcoin was merely soft. Whatever hit it, it was not crypto beta. It was
name-specific selling, and it happened on 4.5 million shares against a 4.2
million average — ordinary participation, no panic, no capitulation. Just supply.

That single ratio — DJT's daily move against a bitcoin ETF — is the cheapest
honest read available on this name. When they move together, you are trading
bitcoin with extra steps. When DJT moves twice as hard, something else is
repricing.

Now look at where the moving averages sit. The **50-day average is 9.071**. The

Above: the **200-day average is 10.164**. The **20-day range high is 10.23**.

*(A moving average is just the average closing price over the last N days —

So DJT is pinned between two converging walls. RSI, which measures whether recent

That is not a trend. That is a spring.

**Above 10.23 the stock clears its 20-day high and its 200-day average in a single
move.** Nothing below that is a signal, and the 52-week range — **$6.96 to
$17.97** — tells you the spring can go a long way once it releases.

## What to watch for

Four things. Three of them are events, one is a level. None of them have happened.

**1. The Truth API customer count on the next call.** Ten customers is a rounding
error. **Fifty customers at the same price is $60 million a year**, which is 2.4%
of the market cap and, more importantly, a *growth line with a number attached*.
That is the single disclosure that would convert this from a meme into a
re-rating. Watch the call, not the headlines.

**2. A docket date on the lawsuit.** Right now the legal risk is a fog. The
moment a court schedules something, the fog becomes a dated binary — a ruling
either kills the one thing this company owns that nobody else does, or blesses
it. Dated binaries can be traded with defined risk. Fog cannot.

**3. A discount to bitcoin plus cash.** If the equity ever trades meaningfully
below the value of what it holds, this stops being a sentiment story and becomes
an entirely different trade — you would be buying assets at a discount and
getting the social network for free. That is a real setup with real precedent,
and it is not the argument anyone is having on the timeline.

**4. The level: 10.23.** A daily close above it clears the 20-day high and the
200-day average together. On the other side, **losing the 20-day low at 7.90
opens the 52-week low at 6.96.** Between those two numbers there is nothing to
do, and doing nothing is the position.

---

## Where I actually stand

No position. No trade.

Not because the idea is stupid — the API is the most interesting thing this
company has ever built, and I spent real time on it before concluding it does not
size. It fails on two numbers. **$12 million of revenue against $2.5 billion of
market cap**, and a stock that fell 5.36% during the single most headline-dense
week of the quarter, when the thesis needed a rip and got a fade.

The tape already ran the experiment. It voted no.

But the setup is worth carrying on a watchlist, because everyone is arguing about
the politics and nobody is watching the customer count. The politics are already
in the price. The subscription line is not.
