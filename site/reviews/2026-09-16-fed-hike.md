## WHAT THE FED DID

The Fed raised rates a quarter point to 3.75-4.00 percent. The vote was 12-0 with no dissents. It is the first hike since July 2023. The statement says inflation "remains elevated" and calls the hike support for "a timelier return" to 2 percent.

The dot plot matters more than the hike, and it came in hawkish. The dot plot is the chart where each Fed official marks where they think rates should end each year. The median for end-2026 is now 4.1, up from 3.8 in June. That means one more quarter-point hike before December. The 2027 median is also 4.1, so no cuts next year. 2028 is 3.9. The official page shows 12 officials at 4.125, 4 at 4.375 (two more hikes) and 2 at 3.875 (done after today). That adds up to 18, and the Fed has 19 members, so treat the exact counts as unverified. Projections: core inflation 3.4 this year, growth 2.3, unemployment 4.1. This is not one-and-done. It is one hike today and one more penciled in.
