## KEEL — the one that cleared

Keel Infrastructure is a bitcoin miner turning itself into an AI and data-centre landlord, about $2 billion in size. It closed at **$3.995**, up 10.7% from yesterday's $3.61, and in doing so took out its highest price of the last 20 sessions on more than one and a half times its normal volume. It is also outrunning the S&P. That is the textbook shape the long screen hunts for.

The spread: **buy the October 2 $4.00 call, sell the October 2 $5.50 call.** It costs **$26 per contract, and that $26 is the absolute most you can lose** — that is the whole point of buying one option and selling another against it. The most it can make is **$124**, so you are risking one dollar to make roughly four and three-quarters. It starts making money above **$4.26**. Eleven contracts fit inside the band at $286 of total risk.

The odds, from KEEL's own history and nothing else: in the 54 times this exact shape has appeared, the stock was higher ten sessions later **59.3% of the time** — but it actually travelled the full distance to the $5.53 target only **9.3% of the time**. I ran that a second way as an independent check (QR-025) and got 59.3% again, on 32 independent events across five years. **Grade A** — that is a real sample spanning several different markets, not a handful of cherry-picked instances. Worth knowing: the average outcome was +9.7% but the worst was −27.2%, and against the S&P it only won 51.9% of the time. It goes up a lot when it goes up, and it beats the index barely more often than a coin flip.

Against the market: the broker prices that $4 call with a **31.8%** chance of profit. My directional number is 59.3%. That 27-point gap is the entire reason this name ranks first. **Expected value works out to +$0.89 per contract, about nine cents a session.** Positive, and thin. The edge is in the direction, not in reaching the target.

On the shares themselves: **stop at $3.48**, which is a full ATR below — that matters, because this desk already paid for that lesson here. Back on 9/10 (row B-327) a KEEL setup got passed on precisely because someone proposed a stop inside a single day's trading range, and on a stock that moves 8% a day that turns a $300 risk budget into a $6,800 position. This stop is 13% below the close and safely outside a normal day. **Target $5.53**, with a shelf at $4.27 on the way.

**The catalyst is what keeps this off the ticket list.** Earnings are clear — the next report is 11/12, well past the October 2 expiry. The insider picture is genuinely good, and it is the strongest thing here: **four open-market buys in 90 days, 155,017 shares, $525,657, and not a single sale.** The CEO bought 58,888 shares at $3.33 with his own money; the COO bought twice, at $3.25 and $3.78. I checked each of those per-share prices against where KEEL actually traded in August — they line up, so this is real and not the known bug where that tool counts another company's filings by mistake.

But the buying is a month old, and **nothing I can see explains today's 10.7% move.** The most recent news article on this company is dated 8/17. A double-digit day with no visible reason is a flag, not a green light. There are dated things pending — permitting at Moses Lake was guided for "late Q3," which is now; a 2-gigawatt Pennsylvania update is guided for December or January; and the stock carries a 10%-plus weight in three State Street ETFs, so fund flows push it around. None of that is confirmed news. **Verdict: STALK, not a candidate. The catalyst gate is owed.**

## GRAB — the thesis is right and the trade is still bad

GRAB closed at **$2.795**, below its 50-day average of $3.47, at the bottom of its 20-session range, on 2.4 times normal volume, and 20 points worse than the S&P over the last month. The bearish story is live and genuine: the stock is down 41% this year, on 9/15 it agreed to pay **$1.49 billion in cash** for 60% of a buy-now-pay-later lender that will not even close until late 2027, and the market sold it on the news. Insiders have sold eight times in 90 days — 781,349 shares, $2.75 million, zero buys — including the CEO dumping 400,000 shares and the CFO selling again **yesterday**.

Everything about that says short. **The options say do not.** The October 2 $3.00/$2.50 put spread costs $22 and can make $28. But in 80 instances of this breakdown shape in GRAB's own history, it reached the $2.31 floor within ten sessions **twice** — a 2.5% hit rate, grade A sample. **Expected value is negative $11.13 per contract.** You would be paying $22 for a shot at $28 on an event that happens one time in forty.

And there is no edge to trade on anyway: the broker prices that put at a 46.1% chance of profit, my number is 46.3%. **A gap of two-tenths of a point.** The market has this priced correctly.

One more thing that softens the bear case: three of the eight insider sales were **identical share counts sold twice** — 30,000 twice, 50,000 twice, 38,000 twice. That is the fingerprint of a pre-scheduled selling plan, not executives losing faith. The tool's "bearish overhang" verdict overstates it.

This desk has now passed on GRAB three times in three days, and the reason keeps being the same: the move has already happened. **Verdict: NO.**
