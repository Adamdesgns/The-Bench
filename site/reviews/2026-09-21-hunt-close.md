## Applied Digital — the best expected value that also passed every gate

APLD closed at $28.195, breaking above the high of its last twenty sessions on heavy volume, and it outran the S&P doing it. That is the shape the long screen looks for.

The structure is an **October 2nd $28/$33 call debit spread**. You pay about **$128 per contract, and $128 is the most you can lose** — that is 42% of the band, so one contract is a comfortable size. The most it can make is **$372**, just under three times the money. You break even at **$29.28**, and you collect the full $372 if APLD is at $33 or higher on October 2nd.

On the odds: I went back through APLD's own history and found 74 times this same setup appeared. In 63.5% of them the stock moved the right way over the next ten sessions, and in 28.4% it went all the way to a target this far out. That earns a **grade B** — a decent sample, not yet proven across enough different market conditions to lean on hard. Robinhood's own chance-of-profit on the long leg is 33.7%. My number is 29 points better than the broker's, and a gap that wide usually means I am seeing something the option pricing is not — or that I am wrong. A separate run of the same question (QR-026) put the median ten-session move at +4.98% with the typical win beating the S&P by 6.5 points.

The catalyst is real and I verified it: Wells Fargo initiated coverage on September 17th with an Overweight rating, a $50 target, and named APLD a "Top Idea." Their case is $36 billion of take-or-pay leases signed with investment-grade hyperscalers, potentially $86 billion over thirty years, and a contracted book they value at roughly $30 a share on its own — above where the stock trades now. APLD also delivered about 75 megawatts at Polaris Forge 1 for CoreWeave ahead of schedule. Earnings are tentatively October 8th, six days *after* this spread expires, so no print lands inside the trade — but it is close enough that you close this before the print, not through it.

**On the shares:** stop at $26.15, target $33.04.

Now the parts I do not like, because they are real. Rothschild & Co Redburn initiated the stock at *Neutral* this very morning. Insiders have sold 75,000 shares on the open market and bought nothing. And APLD is still trading below its 50-day, 100-day and 200-day averages after a death cross in August — so this is a bounce inside a downtrend, not a trend that is already working. Your break-even at $29.28 sits just above $29, which is the level the tape has been rejecting.

## Super Micro — the most trustworthy odds, the thinnest edge

SMCI closed at $41.185, up 3.5% on the day, cleared its twenty-session high, and unlike APLD it is above *all four* of its major moving averages with a golden cross from August. Technically this is the healthier chart of the two.

The structure is an **October 2nd $41/$47 call debit spread**. It costs about **$170 per contract, which is the maximum loss** — 56% of the band. Maximum gain **$430**, two and a half times the money. Break-even **$42.70**, full payout at $47 or above.

This one has the best evidence grade on the board: 82 historical instances, **grade A**, meaning a big sample spread across several different market environments. And the honest reading of that sample is sobering — direction was a **coin flip, 50/50**, and the stock reached a target this far out only 25.6% of the time. The deeper run (QR-027) is blunter still: across 46 independent instances the *median* ten-session outcome was **-0.12%**. The average is positive only because a handful of huge winners drag it up. The broker's chance-of-profit is 33.3%, so I am 17 points ahead of it — but on a grade-A sample that says "coin flip," that edge is thinner than APLD's on paper.

The catalyst checks out: a roughly $60 billion order book supporting fiscal 2027 revenue guidance of $65–72 billion, reiterated with the August 11th quarter. At the Goldman conference on September 10th their SVP of Corporate Development called demand "pretty excellent," sized the AI-infrastructure market at $2–4 trillion with SMCI holding about 10%, and described capacity of roughly 6,000 racks a month with more coming in Johor. Earnings are November 3rd, a full month past expiry — nothing lands inside this trade.

**On the shares:** stop at $38.32, target $47.00.

The counter-case is louder here than on APLD. **The street's average price target is $39.50 — below today's close.** Goldman has an outright Sell at $34. Insiders sold 400,000 shares for $15.44 million into the $36.60–$40.00 range and bought nothing; I checked those per-share prices against SMCI's actual range and the read holds. MACD has rolled under its signal line even as price made the high. And your break-even of $42.70 is just above the $42 resistance. You are buying a breakout that the analysts who cover it think is already expensive.

There is one piece of history worth naming. Back on August 11th this desk logged an SMCI call debit spread as a bet and it passed — not because the thesis broke, but because nobody re-priced it on the day it had to be decided (B-068). That spread was scoped at one price and decided at another. Today's numbers are live marks pulled after the close, which is exactly what that lesson asked for.
